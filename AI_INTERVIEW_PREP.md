## 1. Focus Area 1: American English Verbal & Written Communication
Since you are being evaluated on **American English (US)** verbal and written fluency, your feedback must be exceptionally clear, well-structured, professional, and grammatically precise.

### Written Feedback structure
Use the **"Positive - Constructive - Actionable"** or **"Claim - Evidence - Impact"** frameworks. Avoid vague adjectives like "good" or "bad."
*   **Vague/Weak Feedback:** *"The second response is bad because it has grammar issues and the tone is weird."*
*   **Strong US Professional Feedback:** *"Model B's output is less effective because it contains several subject-verb agreement errors (e.g., 'the data show' instead of 'the data shows'). Additionally, the tone shifts from professional to overly informal in the final paragraph, which violates the user's constraint for a business-oriented response."*

### Key Written & Verbal Communication Rules
*   **Active Voice:** Use active voice over passive voice. (e.g., *"Model A generated a concise summary"* instead of *"A concise summary was generated by Model A"*).
*   **Signposting:** Use transition words to guide the evaluator through your reasoning (*First, Furthermore, Consequently, On the other hand, Specifically*).
*   **Tone:** Maintain an objective, analytical, and professional demeanor. Speak and write as an expert consultant or quality auditor.
*   **Diction & Punctuation (US Standard):**
    *   Use American spelling (e.g., *analyze, behavior, color, program* instead of *analyse, behaviour, colour, programme*).
    *   Use the **Oxford Comma** (e.g., *Model A, Model B, and Model C*).
    *   Place punctuation inside quotation marks (e.g., *"excellent,"* rather than *"excellent",*).

## 2. Focus Area 2: LLM Prompting & Comparative Evaluation (ChatGPT vs Claude)
A major part of the assessment evaluates your ability to compare model outputs and write effective prompts.

### Claude vs. ChatGPT: Core Comparison Matrix

| Dimension | Anthropic Claude (e.g., Claude 3.5 Sonnet) | OpenAI ChatGPT (e.g., GPT-4o / o1) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Strength** | Superior logical reasoning, long-context comprehension, nuanced academic/literary tone, excellent XML/Markdown formatting, and precise instruction-following over long outputs. | High versatility, excellent multi-modal capabilities (DALL-E, Advanced Voice), fast narrative generation, extensive web-browsing capabilities, and highly structured action-oriented guides. |
| **Writing Style & Tone** | Nuanced, objective, human-like, conversational yet intellectually rigorous, less prone to cliché patterns. | Direct, enthusiastic, structured with bullet points, sometimes uses predictable AI transition phrases (e.g., *"Delve deep," "In conclusion," "It is important to note"*). |
| **Code & Technical Analysis** | Exceptional at deep codebase-wide understanding, multi-file reasoning, and strict syntax adherence. | Highly proficient, offers fast inline code solutions, excellent at debugging and suggesting modern libraries/frameworks. |
| **Context Handling** | Very strong at retrieving precise details from extremely large contexts (up to 200k tokens) without "forgetting" instructions midway. | Strong, but long context chains may occasionally experience "lost in the middle" phenomena, where instructions in the middle of a prompt are overlooked. |
| **Inherent Bias & Guardrails** | Emphasizes safety, helpfulness, and harmlessness (Constitutional AI), occasionally resulting in slightly cautious refusals for ambiguous requests. | Highly direct, usually attempts to answer immediately, but can sometimes be overly confident or prone to mild hallucination under complex constraints. |

### Comparative Evaluation Strategy
When evaluating ChatGPT vs. Claude side-by-side on a prompt, structure your comparison using these distinct axes:
1.  **Instruction Following (Hard Constraints):** Did both models satisfy all requirements? (e.g., word count, format, specific keywords, perspective).
2.  **Truthfulness & Factuality:** Are there any logical fallacies, math errors, code bugs, or factual hallucinations?
3.  **Clarity & Tone:** Is the text natural, engaging, and appropriate for the target audience? Does it sound artificial?
4.  **Formatting & Structure:** Did the model use tables, Markdown headers, or lists correctly to enhance readability?

## 3. Focus Area 3: Analytical Reasoning, Critical Thinking & Attention to Detail
Evaluators want to see *how* you think. You need to demonstrate rigorous attention to detail by catching subtle errors.

### Techniques for Spotting Subtle Errors
*   **Verify Calculations:** Never assume a math calculation or code snippet in an LLM output is correct. Manually verify or run mental checks.
*   **Scan for Hallucinations:** Check references, quotes, or historical facts. If an LLM cites a specific URL, academic paper, or law, check its validity or flag it as potentially hallucinated if it seems fictitious.
*   **Identify Logical Inconsistencies:** Look for contradictory claims within the same response. (e.g., a model arguing that "Option A is the most cost-effective choice" in paragraph 1, but concluding "Option B offers the lowest overall cost" in paragraph 3).
*   **Check for Edge Cases:** If a prompt asks for a solution to a problem, assess whether the models accounted for edge cases (e.g., empty inputs, negative numbers, extreme conditions).

## 4. Focus Area 4: Screen/Audio Recording Workflow & Technical Setup
Many remote assessments require you to record your screen and audio while completing tasks. Managing this setup smoothly prevents anxiety and technical failure.

### Technical & Environmental Prep
*   **Hardware Check:** Ensure your microphone is positioned correctly (not too close to your mouth to prevent pop/breath sounds). Use a wired headset if possible to minimize audio lag.
*   **Quiet Environment:** Close windows, turn off fans/AC units, and silence notifications on all devices.
*   **Clean Screen Workspace:** Close unrelated browser tabs, private messages, bookmarks, and personal folders before sharing your screen.
*   **Pacing & Breath Control:** Speak at a steady, deliberate pace (around 130–150 words per minute). Pause slightly between major points to gather your thoughts instead of using filler words (*"um," "uh," "like," "you know"*).

### Speak-Aloud Narrative Technique (The "Think-Aloud" Protocol)
Interviewers love when you explicitly narrate your thoughts. It makes your analytical process visible:
*   *Instead of working in silence:* Speak your thoughts.
*   *Example script:* *"I am currently reading the user prompt, which asks both models to write a Python script utilizing list comprehensions to filter out odd numbers. First, looking at Model A's output, I notice that it successfully implemented the list comprehension. However, moving to Model B, it used a standard `for` loop instead, which fails to satisfy the prompt's hard constraint..."*

## 5. Focus Area 5: Guideline Adherence & Quality-Oriented Evaluation
In AI annotation and evaluation, **the guidelines are law**. Adherence is the primary signal of a high-quality evaluator.

### Core Quality Evaluation Checklist
*   **Zero Tolerance for Assumptions:** Do not infer what a guideline "probably" means. Follow the literal wording of the instructions.
*   **Apply Multi-Criteria Rating Scales Correctly:**
    *   **Excellent (L5):** Satisfies all constraints perfectly, highly accurate, engaging tone, formatted beautifully, absolute absence of flaws.
    *   **Good (L4):** Follows major instructions, minor stylistic or formatting suggestions could improve it, zero factual or logical errors.
    *   **Fair (L3):** Follows the gist but missed a minor constraint or has a slightly awkward/artificial tone.
    *   **Poor (L2):** Major instruction-following failure, contain hallucinated information, or contains serious logical gaps.
    *   **Unusable (L1):** Fails to address the prompt entirely, contains dangerous content, or is highly buggy/gibberish.
*   **Justify Every Rating:** When assigning a score, you must point to direct evidence in the text. Refer to the specific line, paragraph, or constraint.

<a name="6-checklist"></a>
## 6. Interactive Interview Quick-Checklist

Before clicking "Start Assessment", run through this checklist:

*   [ ] **Microphone Quality:** Did you record a quick 5-second test on your system to ensure your voice is crisp and clear?
*   [ ] **Workspace Hygiene:** Are all non-essential tabs, Slack/Discord, and personal information closed or hidden?
*   [ ] **Quiet Space:** Is your door locked, phone on "Do Not Disturb", and family/housemates informed not to disturb you for the next 45 minutes?
*   [ ] **Diction Check:** Am I ready to use American English spelling, active voice, and structured transitions?
*   [ ] **Evaluation Framework Mindset:** Am I ready to evaluate outputs on: 1. Instruction Following, 2. Factuality, 3. Style/Tone, 4. Formatting?
*   [ ] **No Fillers:** Am I conscious of avoiding "uh/um"? (Remember: silence is better than filler words!)

Good luck with your interview! Use your analytical reasoning, stay calm, speak clearly, and strictly follow every instruction given on-screen.
